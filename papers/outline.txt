DeepLatent pitch

Latent factor models are common in the social sciences, 
but can be hard to estimate when the number of observations 
or dimensionality of the objects studied grow.

We propose a class of latent factor models that offer:

    - Handling metadata for more expressive models and supervised learning
    ==> covariates + outcomes 

    - Joint modeling of any data source 
    ==> different data sources used for inference

    - Amortized inference 
    ==> speed and scalability + transfer learning + interpretability + model sharing across contexts

Theoretical analysis:

    - The class of models we consider and typical special cases
        ==> Probabilistic PCA / Basic topic models / Deeper models
    - Estimation via the extended VAE (heuristics and theoretical foundations)
        - Heuristic view: Regularized autoencoder architecture
        - Theoretical justification: Relationship to variational inference and the ELBO
        - Extensions to metadata and multiple modalities
        - Estimation algorithm: Scaling properties (complexity of the estimation algorithm O(something))
    - ELBO and analysis of the approximation error relative to the marginal likelihood
        - formalize the proofs of the universal approximators to decrease (i) the variational and (ii) the amortization gap
    - General estimation rate guarantees for the target density and the latent factors 
    (parametric and non-parametric settings for the decoder, only non-parametric setting for the encoders of course)
    - Identifiability: Discuss lack of identifiability, particularly as the models become "deep". 
        ==> Discuss identifiability in well-studied linear models (PPCA)
        ==> Explain the diffeomorphism story for deeper models.
        ==> Provide simulation evidence that it doesn't seem to be such a big deal 
            (perhaps because of implicit regularization in deep neural nets)
    - Theory vs. Practice (and common use cases)
        - you can optimize surrogate objectives for estimation stability via beta-VAE for instance 
        (e.g., beta <1 avoids posterior collapse) or prediction tasks (e.g., business cycles / conflicts)
        ==> our view is that, ultimately, what matters is "good" latent factors
        ==> the latent factors are best viewed as social constructs that require human validation, 
        and inference should not necessarily be done on the DGP (which we don't really believe in to begin with)
        - cross-modality training 
        ==> you can also encode/decode different things (e.g., texts to votes)
        - you can do transfer learning via pre-trained embeddings
        ==> weaker theoretical guarantees
